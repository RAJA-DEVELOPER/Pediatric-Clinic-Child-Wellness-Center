1
Registration & Medical History Review

Our nurse reviews your child's health records, previous growth data, and any concerns you'd like to discuss with the doctor. Takes about 5–10 minutes.

2
Physical Measurements

Height, weight, head circumference (if applicable), and BMI are measured using certified, calibrated equipment. All measurements are recorded digitally.

3
Developmental Milestone Screening

Dr. Sharma uses structured observation and standardized questionnaires to assess your child's development across four key domains: gross motor, fine motor, language, and social-emotional.

4
Chart Analysis & Interpretation

Results are plotted on WHO-standard growth charts. The doctor explains what each percentile means for your child and whether any follow-up is needed.

5
Guidance, Next Steps & Report

You receive a personalized report with action items, referral letters (if needed), dietary recommendations, and your next follow-up appointment date.

Newborns (0–3 months)

Tracking birth weight recovery, feeding patterns, and early reflexes. Crucial for detecting early complications.

Infants (3–12 months)

Rapid growth phase monitoring, head circumference tracking, motor milestone screening, and introduction to solid foods guidance.

Toddlers (1–3 years)

Language development screening, walking/running assessment, and behavioural milestone checks. Autism screening recommended at 18 & 24 months.

Preschool (3–5 years)

School-readiness assessments, fine motor skill checks, speech clarity review, and social interaction patterns.

School-Age (6–12 years)

BMI monitoring, puberty preparation, attention and learning milestone checks, and mental well-being screening.

Adolescents (13–18 years)

Puberty progress tracking, BMI & nutritional counselling, mental health check-ins, and academic stress assessment.

What to Bring
  • Child's immunization / vaccination record book
  • Previous growth charts (if available)
  • List of current medications or supplements
  • Your child's health insurance card
  • Any concerns or questions written down
  • Comfort item for young children (toy, blanket)
Fasting Requirements

No fasting is required for a standard growth monitoring visit.

If Blood Tests Are Included Infants under 1 year: 2 hours fast. Children 1–5 years: 4 hours fast. Children 6+ years: 6–8 hours fast. Plain water is always allowed.
Clothing Tips
  • Dress your child in easy-to-remove clothing (no buttons or ties)
  • Lightweight, comfortable clothes for accurate weight measurement
  • Remove shoes and heavy accessories before measurement
  • Hair down / loose for head circumference accuracy
Preparing Your Child
  • Explain the visit in simple, positive terms
  • Bring a favourite toy or book
  • Schedule when your child is well-rested
  • Avoid scheduling during nap time for young children
  • Arrive 10 minutes early to allow settling time

Understanding Your Child's Growth Chart

Learn how to read percentiles and what they mean for your child's health journey

What Do Percentiles Mean?

A percentile indicates how your child compares to others of the same age and sex. A child at the 50th percentile for weight means 50% of children weigh more and 50% weigh less.

What's "Normal"? Any percentile between the 5th and 95th is considered healthy. It's the trend over time that matters most — not a single measurement.

When to Be Concerned Sudden drop of 2+ percentile channels, consistently below 3rd or above 97th percentile, or measurements not matching the child's genetic potential (parent heights).
