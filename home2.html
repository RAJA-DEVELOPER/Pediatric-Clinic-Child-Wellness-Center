Tracking Your Child's Development

Understanding what's typical at each stage helps you celebrate progress and spot any concerns early.

Physical Growth

  • 0–3 moGains 150–210g/week
  • 6 moBirth weight doubles
  • 1 yrBirth weight triples
  • 2 yrsApprox. half adult height
  • 5 yrsSteady 5–6 cm/year growth

Cognitive Development

  • 2 moTracks objects with eyes
  • 6 moExplores objects by mouth
  • 1 yrObject permanence achieved
  • 2 yrsSymbolic & pretend play
  • 5 yrsBasic reading & math skills

Social & Emotional

  • 1 moResponds to caregiver voice
  • 2 moFirst social smile
  • 9 moStranger anxiety begins
  • 2 yrsParallel play with peers
  • 4 yrsCooperative play & empathy

Language & Communication

  • 2 moCooing & vowel sounds
  • 6 moBabbling (ba-ba, da-da)
  • 1 yrFirst meaningful words
  • 2 yrs50+ words, 2-word phrases
  • 5 yrsFull sentences, storytelling

When Should I Be Concerned?

Most babies smile socially between 6–8 weeks. If your baby hasn't smiled by 3 months, it's worth a check-up with our pediatrician. It could be related to vision, hearing, or developmental factors — all of which we can assess early for the best outcomes.

Most toddlers speak in 2-word phrases by 24 months and simple sentences by 36 months. If your child isn't hitting these markers, our speech therapy referral program can help evaluate and address language delays early.

Some attention challenges are developmentally normal, but persistent difficulty focusing, impulsiveness, or hyperactivity warrants assessment. Our school-age ADHD screening program uses validated tools and a multidisciplinary approach to provide accurate diagnosis and personalized support plans.

We recommend checkups at: 1 week, 1 month, 2 months, 4 months, 6 months, 9 months, 12 months, 15 months, 18 months, 24 months, then annually from age 3 onward. Each visit includes developmental screening, vaccinations if due, and parent guidance.
